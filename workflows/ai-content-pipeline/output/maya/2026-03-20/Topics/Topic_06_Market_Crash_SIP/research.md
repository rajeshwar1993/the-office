# Research: Markets Crashed — Should You Panic-Sell or Double Down on Your SIP?

## Topic Overview
Addresses retail investor panic during the current FII-driven market sell-off. Uses historical crash data to show why continuing SIPs during downturns produces better long-term outcomes through rupee-cost averaging.

## Key Data Points

### Current Market Context (March 2026)
- Sensex down ~2,500 points from recent peak
- FII net outflows: ~Rs 60,000 crore in recent weeks
- Trigger: global oil price surge, geopolitical uncertainty, dollar strength
- Retail investor panic: SIP stoppage rates climbing (AMFI data)
- Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) absorbing significant FII sell-off

| 2008 GFC | 21,000 → 8,000 (-62%) | ~2 years to prior peak | +30-40% by recovery |

### Rupee-Cost Averaging Mechanics
- SIP at Rs 10,000/month in Nifty index fund
- At Nifty 22,000 (pre-crash NAV ~200): 50 units purchased
- At Nifty 17,000 (post-crash NAV ~155): 64.5 units purchased (~29% more)
- Extra units compound during recovery, amplifying returns
- The math works because SIP is systematic — removes timing decisions

### SIP Stoppage Behaviour
- AMFI data shows SIP stoppages spike 15-25% during major corrections
- Most stoppages happen within 2-3 months of crash onset
- Investors who stop SIPs typically don't restart until markets fully recover — missing the entire recovery rally
- Behavioural bias: loss aversion is ~2x stronger than equivalent gain satisfaction

### Unrealised vs Realised Loss
- Key investor education point: portfolio showing -20% is unrealised (paper loss)
- Loss only materialises when investor sells
- Units remain intact regardless of current market price
- Recovery restores value of existing units — but only for those who held

### FII vs DII Dynamics
- FIIs are net sellers due to: dollar strengthening, oil-driven risk-off, better yields in US/EU
- DIIs (mutual funds, insurance, pension) are net buyers — absorbing FII exits
- SIP contributions form a large chunk of DII buying power
- Indian retail investor base has matured significantly since 2020

## Trend Relevance (March 2026)
- Oil crisis creating cascading effects: inflation up, rupee weakening, FII outflows
- This crash is structural (oil/geopolitical) rather than systemic (banking/financial crisis)
- Structural corrections historically recover faster than systemic crises
- SIP stoppage risk is highest in the first 4-6 weeks of a correction — making this content time-sensitive
