# Research: Crude Oil Just Hit $100 — Here's How It's Quietly Wrecking Your Budget

## Topic Overview
Iran-Middle East escalation has pushed crude oil past $100/barrel. India, which imports 88% of its crude oil, is seeing cascading effects: rupee at record low (92.94), FII outflows of ~₹60,000 crore, and Sensex crash of 2,500 points.

## Key Facts & Data Points

### Oil Price & Geopolitics
- Crude oil crossed $100/barrel due to Iran-Middle East escalation
- India imports 88% of its crude oil — one of the highest import dependencies globally
- Higher oil = higher dollar outflow = weaker rupee

### Rupee Impact
- INR/USD at 92.94 — record low
- Weak rupee makes ALL imports expensive, not just oil
- Electronics, chemicals, industrial raw materials — all priced in dollars
- Even "Made in India" products use imported raw materials

### Stock Market & FII Flows
- FIIs (Foreign Institutional Investors) pulled ~₹60,000 crore from Indian markets
- Sensex dropped 2,500 points
- During global risk events, foreign money exits "growth bets" (India) for safe havens (US treasuries, gold)
- Historical precedent: similar FII exits in 2018 and 2022 oil shocks; markets recovered in both cases

### Food Inflation Chain
- Oil → diesel → transport costs → food prices
- All food in India transported primarily by truck (diesel-dependent)
- Price increases passed from transporters → wholesalers → retailers → consumers
- Indirect connection not always visible in headlines

### EMI & Interest Rate Impact
- Oil-driven inflation forces RBI to consider rate hikes
- 2022 precedent: RBI raised repo rate by 250 basis points over 18 months
- Impact on 50 lakh home loan: ~₹2,000-3,000 additional EMI per month
- Affects all floating-rate loans: home, car, personal, credit card interest

### RBI's Dilemma
- Option 1: Raise rates → controls inflation but increases EMI burden
- Option 2: Hold rates → keeps EMIs stable but inflation runs higher
- Either way, consumers bear the cost
